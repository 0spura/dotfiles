# Tax Considerations for Brazilian Equity Investors

> **Disclaimer:** tax rules change. Always verify current legislation with a certified accountant (contador) or tax advisor before making decisions based on this reference. This is a framework for awareness, not professional tax advice.

---

## Capital Gains Tax on Equities (Renda Variável)

### The R$20,000 Monthly Exemption
Individual investors in Brazil are **exempt from capital gains tax** on equity sales if total sales in a calendar month do not exceed **R$20,000**.

- This applies to **common stocks on B3** (not day trades, not derivatives).
- The R$20,000 threshold is based on **total sales value**, not gains. If you sell R$18,000 of stock that cost you R$15,000, the R$3,000 gain is fully exempt.
- Above R$20,000 in monthly sales: **15% tax on the net gain** (sell price minus acquisition cost).

**Practical implication for portfolio management:** when exiting a meaningful position, consider spreading sales across multiple months to stay below the threshold. On a R$5,000 gain with a 15% tax rate, this saves R$750 — worth planning for.

### Day Trade Exception
Day trades (buy and sell the same asset on the same day) are taxed at **20%** on gains with **no exemption**, regardless of amount. This framework does not involve day trading — but avoid accidentally triggering it.

## Loss Harvesting (Compensação de Prejuízos)

Realized losses in equities can be used to offset gains in the **same or future months** with no expiration limit.

**How it works:**
- Month 1: sell Stock A at a R$2,000 loss → carry forward R$2,000 loss.
- Month 2: sell Stock B at a R$3,000 gain → taxable gain = R$3,000 − R$2,000 = R$1,000.
- Tax due: R$1,000 × 15% = R$150 (instead of R$450 without the offset).

**Losses can only offset gains in the same category:** variable income losses offset variable income gains. They cannot offset fixed income gains (e.g., CDB interest).

**Record-keeping:** you are responsible for tracking your own cost basis and accumulated losses. B3 does not do this for you. Use a brokerage with a built-in IRPF tool, or track manually in a spreadsheet.

## Dividends and JCP (Juros sobre Capital Próprio)

### Dividends
Currently **exempt from income tax** for individual investors in Brazil. When a company declares dividends, you receive the full amount with no withholding.

> Note: Brazilian tax legislation has periodically discussed taxing dividends. Monitor for changes.

### JCP (Juros sobre Capital Próprio)
An alternative to dividends. Companies can distribute JCP as a way to reduce their corporate tax burden.

- **For the investor:** JCP is subject to **15% withholding tax at source**. The company withholds and remits; you receive the net amount.
- **Practical difference:** if a company pays R$1.00 per share as JCP, you receive R$0.85. If it pays as dividends, you receive R$1.00.
- Both show up in your brokerage account as "proventos" — check the type to know the tax treatment.

## Monthly Tax Reporting (DARF)

When you have taxable gains in a month (sales above R$20,000 with net gain), you must:
1. Calculate the net gain yourself.
2. Apply the 15% rate.
3. Pay via **DARF** (Documento de Arrecadação de Receitas Federais) by the **last business day of the following month**.

Late payment incurs interest (Selic rate) and a fine (0.33%/day up to 20%).

**Tool:** the Receita Federal's e-CAC portal or your brokerage's IRPF module can generate the DARF. Do not rely on the brokerage to file it for you — you are responsible.

## Annual Income Tax Declaration (IRPF)

All equity positions must be declared annually in your IRPF:
- **"Bens e Direitos":** declare each position at **acquisition cost** (not market value). This is your cost basis for future capital gains calculations.
- **"Rendimentos Isentos":** declare exempt dividends received during the year.
- **"Rendimentos Sujeitos à Tributação Exclusiva":** declare JCP received (already taxed at source).
- **"Renda Variável":** declare monthly gains/losses and tax paid via DARF.

**Key point:** if you buy the same stock multiple times at different prices, your cost basis is the **weighted average acquisition price** across all purchases. Track this in your brokerage or a spreadsheet.

---

## Tax Efficiency Rules of Thumb

1. **Stay below R$20,000/month in sales** whenever possible — the exemption is valuable.
2. **Harvest losses actively** — a realized loss has immediate value as a future tax offset.
3. **Prefer dividend-paying companies over JCP-heavy ones** if tax efficiency matters — dividends are currently tax-free, JCP is not.
4. **Never let tax drive a fundamentally wrong decision** — holding a deteriorating position to avoid realizing a gain is more expensive than paying the tax and rotating into a better asset.
5. **Track cost basis from day one** — reconstructing it years later is painful and error-prone.
