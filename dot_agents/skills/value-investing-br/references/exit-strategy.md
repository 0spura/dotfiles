# When and How to Exit a Position

## The Core Principle

Buying is governed by the 5 filters. Selling is governed by **one question:**

> Has the reason you bought this company stopped being true?

If the answer is no — fundamentals intact, earnings still growing — the correct action is hold, or contribute more if underweight. Selling because price dropped is the most common and most costly mistake in long-term investing.

---

## Valid Reasons to Exit

### 1. Filter 2 Failure: Recurring Earnings Deterioration
The clearest exit signal. Criteria:
- Recurring earnings declined YoY in **2+ consecutive quarters**, AND
- The decline is structural (rising costs, falling revenue, market share loss) — not one-off (a single bad quarter from an exceptional event).

One bad quarter is not an exit. Two consecutive quarters of operational deterioration with no credible recovery path is.

**Process:** before exiting, verify whether management has acknowledged the problem and provided a specific, time-bound recovery plan. Vague statements ("we remain confident") do not count. Concrete guidance revisions with operational actions do.

### 2. Filter 3 Failure: Leverage Becoming Structural
- Net Debt/EBITDA rising above 4.0x with no new revenue-generating assets to show for it.
- Interest coverage falling below 2x.
- Management refinancing debt at materially higher rates without corresponding revenue growth.

Leverage during an investment cycle (building new assets) is expected and not an exit signal. Leverage rising because earnings are falling is.

### 3. Filter 1 Failure: Governance Change
- Company restructures share classes and loses 100% Tag Along protection.
- Change of control that disadvantages minority shareholders.
- Material related-party transactions benefiting the controlling shareholder at the expense of the company.

These are immediate exit signals regardless of earnings quality.

### 4. Better Opportunity in the Same Sector
Not a standalone reason to exit, but valid when combined with a weakening thesis:
- Another company in the same sector passes all 5 filters more cleanly.
- The current position's valuation has stretched significantly above historical sector range.
- Rotating into the stronger company improves portfolio quality without increasing risk.

**Never rotate purely on valuation.** A quality company at 14x P/E in a sector that normally trades at 10–12x is worth monitoring — not selling — if earnings are still growing.

---

## Invalid Reasons to Exit (Common Mistakes)

- **Price dropped X%** → price and value are different things. If fundamentals are intact, a price drop is a discount, not a signal.
- **Macro news is bad** → interest rate fears, currency moves, election results, geopolitical events do not change a company's recurring earnings power.
- **"I need the money"** → this is why the emergency reserve exists. If you are selling equities for liquidity needs, the reserve was not adequate.
- **Another stock looks more exciting** → excitement is not a filter. Run the 5 filters on both and compare objectively.

## How to Exit: Practical Considerations

### Tax Efficiency (Brazil)
- Sales of Brazilian equities up to **R$20,000/month** are **exempt from capital gains tax** for individual investors (swing trades and day trades excluded).
- Above R$20,000/month in sales: 15% tax on gains (variable income).
- **Loss harvesting:** realized losses can offset gains in the same or future months. Track losses — they have value.
- **Practical implication:** if you need to exit a large position, spreading sales across multiple months below the R$20,000 threshold can eliminate tax entirely.

### Sequencing
When rotating out of one position and into another:
1. Exit the weaker position first (or in tranches if large).
2. Verify the target position still passes all 5 filters at the time of entry — not just when you first analyzed it.
3. Do not rush the entry to "catch up" to a price. The process is mechanical.

### Partial vs. Full Exit
- If the thesis is weakening but not fully broken (e.g., one quarter of earnings decline with management acknowledging and addressing it): **reduce position size**, do not fully exit.
- If Filter 1 or governance has broken: **full exit**. Governance failures tend to compound.
- If leverage is rising but new assets are being built: **hold and monitor** for 2 quarters before deciding.
