# Line Editing

The sentence-level pass of the draft phase in [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). The skeleton settled the argument and the draft turned each assertion into a paragraph. This pass makes each sentence land the way the reader expects, and strips the mechanical phrasing that reads as machine-written. Run it once the paragraphs exist, not before, because rewording prose you may still cut is wasted work.

## Reader expectation, from Gopen and Swan

Readers expect certain information in certain places, and prose flows when you honor those slots.

- **Topic position, the start of the sentence.** Open with information the reader already has, the thread carried from the previous sentence. This is the given-new contract. Known first, new last, so each sentence hands off to the next.
- **Stress position, the end of the sentence.** Land the new, the important, the thing you want emphasized at the end, where the reader naturally gives weight. Do not bury the point in the middle and trail off into a qualifier.
- **Subject and verb close together.** Keep the actor next to its action. A long clause wedged between them makes the reader hold the subject in memory, which is where scientific prose most often loses its reader.
- **One unit of discourse, one point.** A sentence that has to carry two ideas usually wants to be two sentences.

## Unbury the verb

Academic prose hides its actions inside nouns, and the result is the abstract, lifeless tone reviewers call mechanical.

- **Nominalizations, the zombie nouns of Helen Sword.** "The instrumentation performs the collection of metrics" hides two verbs inside nouns. Free them. "The instrumentation collects metrics." Watch for endings like -tion, -ment, -ance, -ility sitting where a verb belongs.
- **Name the actor.** Prefer a real subject doing a real thing over an abstract entity that a process happens to. The sentence gets shorter and truer at once.

## Paramedic Method, from Lanham

A mechanical revision drill for a sentence that feels swollen.

1. Circle the prepositions. Chains like "in the context of the analysis of" are where words go to die, so cut them.
2. Circle the "is, are, was, were" forms. A weak linking verb often hides the real action nearby.
3. Find the action. Ask who is doing what, and put that actor in the subject and that action in the verb.
4. Start fast. Cut the slow wind-up ("It is important to note that", "This paper presents an approach that") and begin at the point.

## The mechanical cadence to break

Apply the global prose-voice rule at the point of use: reread each paragraph for the one failure it names, a stock mark or scaffold repeated until the reader feels the pattern, and rewrite any sentence that recurs.
